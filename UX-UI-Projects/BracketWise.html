Overview

BracketWise is a Canadian income tax optimization web application for individuals with multi-stream income. It accepts T4 employment, self-employment, capital gains, and other income, applies CRA-accurate CPP tier rules, EI premiums, and federal and provincial tax brackets — then runs an iterative optimization engine to recommend the ideal split between RRSP, FHSA, and TFSA contributions. Users see a clear before-and-after view of their tax picture, April obligations, and take-home income.

  • Impact

    Designed and developed a fully functional, CRA-accurate tax optimization tool from concept to working application. The engine handles the self-referential nature of RRSP optimization through a 5-pass convergence algorithm, surfacing contribution recommendations that general-purpose calculators miss — particularly for Canadians juggling T4, self-employment, and capital gains income.

  • Hats Worn

Empathize

Canadians with T4 employment plus self-employment, capital gains, or other income face layers of compounding tax complexity that standard calculators don't address. CPP spans two tiers with different rates and different treatment for employed versus self-employed individuals. The 2024 capital gains inclusion rate change added a second threshold. Most critically, RRSP optimization is self-referential: contributing reduces taxable income, which reduces tax owing, which frees up more cash to contribute — a loop no single-pass calculator resolves correctly.

To understand the real-world friction, we identified the core pain points users face when trying to make these decisions on their own:

  • Standard calculators don't model SE CPP correctly — they miss the employer half and Tier 2, understating the April liability for self-employed users.
  • RRSP contribution advice typically ignores cash-flow constraints, leaving users at risk of a larger-than-expected balance owing in April.
  • The dual benefit of FHSA — deductible contributions and tax-free withdrawals — is rarely surfaced together, so many eligible users under-utilize it.
  • Mixed-income users don't know their true marginal rate. The T4-only rate chart gives the wrong answer once self-employment or capital gains enter the picture.

For mixed-income Canadians, finding the optimal RRSP contribution means solving a circular dependency: the right amount depends on the tax savings, which depend on the amount.

Market Research

Several tools exist for Canadian tax filing and basic planning — Wealthsimple Tax, TurboTax, TaxTips.ca, and SimpleTax — but none optimizes registered contributions within a cash constraint or resolves the RRSP self-reference problem iteratively. BracketWise differentiates by handling all four income streams with correct CRA mechanics, solving the contribution optimization loop, surfacing the provincial tax-free income floor, and separating April liabilities from general tax owing so users can plan their cash position accurately.

Main Features

Multi-Stream Tax Engine

BracketWise handles T4 employment, self-employment, capital gains (including the 2024 inclusion rate change), and other income. Each stream triggers different CPP tier calculations, EI rules, and deduction lines — all resolved automatically so users enter their income and get accurate results without needing to know which CRA line each item falls under.

5-Pass Contribution Optimizer

RRSP optimization is self-referential — contributing reduces tax owing, which frees more cash to contribute. BracketWise solves this with a 5-pass convergence loop that stabilizes on the correct amount. FHSA is always filled first, since its contributions are both deductible and withdrawn tax-free. RRSP is then maximized within the user's cash budget and a provincial tax-free income floor.

Before & After Dashboard

Users see their tax picture before and after optimization side by side: total tax savings, April liability, effective rate, marginal rate, and take-home income. The April liability is shown separately from total tax owing — distinguishing the balance due from what the employer already withheld — so users can plan their cash position and avoid surprises at tax time.

Defining Principles

To align design and development decisions, we identified the key rules and interactions the application needed to model correctly:

  • How do CPP Tier 1 and Tier 2 interact when the user has both T4 and self-employment income? (T4 income consumes YMPE/YAMPE room first)
  • Which registered account should be filled first: FHSA, RRSP, or TFSA?
  • How do we keep the April cash liability visible so users don't over-contribute and be caught short at tax time?
  • What is the correct tax-free income floor per province, and how does it constrain RRSP contributions?
  • How do we communicate the self-referential nature of RRSP optimization without confusing users?
  • How should effective vs. marginal rate be displayed for mixed-income users, where the T4-only rate chart gives the wrong answer?
  • How should an employer RRSP match affect available contribution room?

User Flow

The flow has three phases. In the Income Input phase, the user enters income across all four streams — T4, self-employment, capital gains, and other — along with their available cash budget, existing RRSP and FHSA room, and any childcare expenses. In the Baseline Calculation phase, the engine computes the unoptimized tax picture: tax owing, take-home income, effective rate, and marginal rate, with no registered contributions assumed. In the Optimization phase, the 5-pass loop converges on the optimal FHSA, RRSP, and TFSA amounts, and the dashboard presents the before-and-after comparison with tax savings and April obligations clearly separated.

Reflections

Lesson Learned

  • Documenting tax rules as design decisions:
    Translating CRA rules into an approachable UI required making many invisible interactions explicit — like the existence of a provincial tax-free floor, or how CPP Tier 2 interacts with self-employment income. Mapping each CRA line to the input it drives and the output it affects kept design and development tightly aligned throughout, and reduced the risk of a mathematically correct but confusing user experience.

If We Had More Time

  • Broader provincial coverage and full TFSA optimization:
    The current engine models federal tax plus BC, Alberta, and Saskatchewan. Expanding to all provinces would make BracketWise useful to a much larger share of Canadians. Additionally, completing TFSA optimization — allocating remaining cash after FHSA and RRSP up to the annual limit — and adding a PDF export of the personalized tax summary would turn the tool into a shareable planning artifact for conversations with financial advisors.
